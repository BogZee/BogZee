# Simulation: Exploring the Information Needed to Master Mathematics

This simulation follows Maya, an aspiring mathematician, as she explores the full landscape of knowledge, habits, and resources required to master mathematics from foundational numeracy to cutting-edge research literacy. The aim is to map the essential information Maya must gather, organize, and internalize.

## 1. Simulation Setup
- **Objective:** Achieve graduate-level mathematical maturity with the ability to solve novel problems, read research papers, and teach core concepts.
- **Time Horizon:** 5-year roadmap with checkpoints every semester.
- **Constraints:** Balancing a 30-hour/week study schedule with part-time teaching duties.
- **Initial Assessment:** Strength in algebraic manipulation but gaps in proofs, statistics, and computational tools.

## 2. Foundational Knowledge Inventory (Year 0–1)
1. **Numerical Fluency**
   - Place value, arithmetic algorithms, fractions, ratios, modular arithmetic.
   - Diagnostic: Complete problem sets from "The Art of Problem Solving: Prealgebra".
   - Resources: AoPS videos, Khan Academy mastery checks, Paul Lockhart's *Measurement* for intuition.
2. **Algebraic Thinking**
   - Linear equations, inequalities, functions, factoring, polynomials.
   - Practice: Weekly mixed-problem drills with progressively increasing difficulty.
   - Conceptual focus: Variable manipulation, inverse operations, symbolic reasoning.
3. **Euclidean Geometry**
   - Congruence, similarity, theorems (Pythagorean, Ceva's, Menelaus's), coordinate geometry.
   - Activities: Dynamic geometry investigations (GeoGebra), proof construction logs.
4. **Introduction to Logic and Proof**
   - Propositional logic, quantifiers, direct/contradiction/induction proofs.
   - Textbook: Velleman's *How to Prove It* with weekly proof-writing workshops.

### Milestone Deliverable
- Compile a **Foundations Portfolio** containing solved problems, error analyses, and proof write-ups reviewed by a mentor. Portfolio rubric includes clarity, rigor, and reflection components.

## 3. Core Undergraduate Sequence (Year 1–3)
1. **Calculus I–III & Real Analysis**
   - Information needs: Limits, derivatives, integrals, sequences, series, continuity, differentiability, measure of rigor.
   - Strategy: Pair computational practice (Stewart's *Calculus*) with theoretical study (Abbott's *Understanding Analysis*).
   - Assessment: Oral exams explaining epsilon–delta proofs and Fourier series convergence criteria.
2. **Linear Algebra**
   - Concepts: Vector spaces, linear transformations, eigenvalues, diagonalization, inner product spaces.
   - Tools: MATLAB/Python notebooks implementing matrix algorithms and visualizations.
   - Output: Concept maps linking abstract and computational perspectives.
3. **Abstract Algebra**
   - Topics: Groups, rings, fields, Galois theory basics.
   - Practice: Weekly theorem presentations, algebra coding projects (permutation group computations).
4. **Probability & Statistics**
   - Probability axioms, random variables, expectation, variance, distributions, inference, Bayesian perspective.
   - Resources: Blitzstein & Hwang's *Introduction to Probability*, ISLR for statistical learning overview.
5. **Discrete Mathematics & Combinatorics**
   - Graph theory, recurrence relations, generating functions, algorithmic complexity.
   - Supplement: Participate in coding contests to reinforce combinatorial reasoning.

### Milestone Deliverable
- **Comprehensive Concept Atlas**: A digital knowledge graph showing dependencies among all core topics, tagged with resource references, summary notes, and sample problems.

## 4. Advanced Specialization Tracks (Year 3–5)
1. **Analysis Track**
   - Functional analysis, measure theory, complex analysis, PDEs.
   - Information sources: Stein & Shakarchi series, Folland's *Real Analysis*, MIT OpenCourseWare lectures.
   - Project: Implement numerical solvers for PDEs and present convergence proofs.
2. **Algebra & Number Theory Track**
   - Modules: Representation theory, algebraic number theory, elliptic curves.
   - Resources: Dummit & Foote, Ireland & Rosen, Silverman's *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*.
   - Deliverable: Seminar paper on modular forms with computational verification using SageMath.
3. **Applied & Computational Track**
   - Focus: Optimization, numerical linear algebra, stochastic processes, machine learning foundations.
   - Tools: Python libraries (NumPy, SciPy, PyTorch), Julia for performance-critical experiments.
   - Capstone: Cross-disciplinary project modeling a real-world system with sensitivity analysis.

### Milestone Deliverable
- **Specialization Thesis**: A publishable-quality paper or software artifact demonstrating mastery of a chosen track, peer-reviewed by faculty or an expert panel.

## 5. Metacognitive & Support Systems
- **Learning Strategies:** Spaced repetition (Anki decks for theorems), deliberate practice sessions, Feynman technique summaries.
- **Community:** Join math circles, attend seminars, participate in online forums (Math StackExchange, Art of Problem Solving).
- **Mentorship:** Quarterly check-ins with advisors, peer study groups, and teaching assistantships to reinforce knowledge through instruction.
- **Time Management:** Semester planners, weekly sprint reviews, Pomodoro-based focus blocks.
- **Well-being:** Scheduled rest, physical exercise, mindfulness to manage cognitive load.

## 6. Information Management Infrastructure
- **Knowledge Base:** Use a digital Zettelkasten (e.g., Obsidian) with structured notes on definitions, theorems, proofs, and connections.
- **Version Control:** Track problem solutions and research notes in Git repositories to maintain history and reproducibility.
- **Data Sources:** Curate datasets for applied math experiments (Kaggle, UCI Repository, open government data).
- **Assessment Dashboard:** Maintain metrics (hours studied, topics mastered, problem success rates) in a spreadsheet or Notion database.

## 8. Risk Management & Feedback Loops
- **Gap Analysis:** Biannual diagnostic exams modeled after Putnam and GRE subject tests.
- **Feedback Sources:** Mentor critiques, peer reviews of proofs, automated grading from MOOCs.
- **Adaptation Plan:** If diagnostics reveal weaknesses, adjust study plan (e.g., insert topology mini-course).
- **Motivation Systems:** Celebrate milestone completions, maintain progress journal, visualize long-term goals.

## 9. Outcomes & Mastery Indicators
- Ability to derive, prove, and communicate complex mathematical ideas clearly.
- Successful completion of graduate-level qualifying exams or equivalent benchmarks.
- Portfolio of research projects, teaching materials, and community contributions.
- Confidence in tackling interdisciplinary problems with mathematical tools.

## 10. Summary of Essential Information Categories
1. **Conceptual Knowledge:** Definitions, theorems, proofs, conceptual frameworks across pure and applied domains.
2. **Procedural Skills:** Problem-solving techniques, computational proficiency, algorithm implementation.
3. **Meta-Learning:** Study strategies, time management, resilience practices.
4. **Community & Communication:** Mentorship, collaboration, teaching experience, presentation skills.
5. **Tools & Resources:** Texts, online lectures, software, datasets, assessment instruments.

By simulating Maya's journey, we expose the full spectrum of information required to master mathematics—grounding study plans in structured phases, informed resource selection, iterative feedback, and sustained metacognitive support.
